## The one open blocker

`gate/land.sh:250-256` increments round and no-progress state only in the
**clone-local** Git common directory. `:293-304` writes it into target history **only
after the candidate has merged**, and `:308-315` rolls a later declared-check failure
back to the pre-merge SHA.

So a reviewed attempt that **fails after counting but before landing** never reaches
target history. A new clone reconstructs the older tracked state (`:212-225`) and can
repeat it. Durable evidence exists for **successful landings only**, while
`instructions/review-policy.md:63-66` and `:79-83` bind the counter to *every reviewed
landing attempt* and claim both measures survive restart and clone.

Round 2's clone-B regression catches an exhausted counter made of three seeded
**landed** rounds — it does not catch the class that matters: attempts that do not
land.

## Two ways forward; the second needs the Human

1. **Persist and reconstruct attempts and no-progress from target history**, not only
   landings — plus a real two-clone fail-before/pass-after lock where attempts fail
   *after* counting and clone B still refuses or parks.
2. **Narrow the binding policy** at `instructions/review-policy.md:63-66,79-83` so the
   durable counter covers landings rather than every reviewed attempt, and narrow the
   acceptance criterion to match. This weakens a rule the operator set, so it is his
   call, not a lane's.

Until one of those lands, the cap remains what it was this morning: an orchestrator
counting by hand. That works exactly as long as the orchestrator is honest and
remembers — which is the condition HR-1726 exists to remove.
