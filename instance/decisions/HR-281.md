## The rules

1. **Parallel fan-out is the default**, not a queue. The orchestrator working
   single-threaded is a violation of `autonomy-and-capacity.md`, not diligence.
2. **Fleet floor is ACTIVE**: `instance/params.yaml: fleet.status: active`,
   floor 6, ceiling 15. It had been sitting at `suspended` as a legacy leftover,
   which the orchestrator treated as licence to work alone.
3. **Below three running lanes → report to the Human unprompted**, saying work
   is thin and more should be queued. Do not wait to be asked.
4. **Report capacity as numbers** ("N lanes running, each doing X"), never as
   prose about intent.
5. **Durable rules go in the REPOSITORY, not agent memory.** `instructions/*.md`
   (loaded every session) and `instance/decisions/HR-*.md` are the homes. His
   reason, and it is correct: anything parked in agent memory is forgotten fast.
   The memory file written during this exchange was deleted; `MEMORY.md` now
   carries a standing do-not-add notice pointing here.

## Why he was right, recorded honestly

Nothing technical was blocking parallelism: 12 cores, 241 GB free, Codex at 92%
unused weekly quota. `autonomy-and-capacity.md` *already* said to keep the fleet
saturated, and the orchestrator did the work inline anyway. The rule existed and
was ignored — which is exactly why enforcement moves from prose to a check.

## Enforcement

`instance/params.yaml: fleet.fleet_idle_check` names the mechanism that fails
closed when the workboard has open rows and zero lanes are running, when the
system lane count is unavailable, or when the open-row count cannot be
determined — so the fleet-idle path does not depend on orchestrator discipline.

**Correction, 2026-08-05 (workboard V3-2.15).** From 2026-07-31 to that date this
section named a state-contract checker under `tools/` that has never existed;
the key pointed at nothing and nothing enforced FLEET-IDLE (audit finding F6,
`instance/audits/false-facts-2026-08-04.md`). The key now names
`orchestrator/fleet/fleet-nudge.sh`, the timer watchdog that actually measures
it, with `daemon/autonomy-keepalive.ts` as the in-daemon backstop. The
requirement above was real; only the mechanism named for it was fictional.

## Superseded in part — the numbers, not the demand (2026-08-05)

Rules 1, 4 and 5 stand unchanged. The fleet **numbers** in rules 2 and 3 are
superseded by [[HR-2342]] (2026-08-04, «Давай там до трьох»), scoped per
repository by [[HR-2398]]:

- **Rule 2** (`floor 6, ceiling 15`; `instance/params.yaml` in fact carried
  `floor: 10`) is replaced by a single `fleet.cap: 3`. HR-2342 states it as a
  ceiling, not a target — "fewer is allowed whenever the work does not need
  them" — so there is no floor key to set. `tools/check-fleet-cap.ts` now
  refuses any value here that disagrees with the ruling.
- **Rule 3** («Коли стає менше трьох паралельних лейнів, уже маєш мені писати»)
  was given when the configured floor was ten, so "fewer than three" then named
  a fleet that had nearly stopped. Under a cap of three it names ordinary
  operation, and reporting it every time is the noise he was woken by. The
  intent is kept and the arithmetic is not: the wake condition is an idle fleet
  with work outstanding. `instructions/autonomy-and-capacity.md` carries that
  reasoning, and this paragraph is the flag that it was changed rather than
  quietly ignored — restoring the literal threshold is his call to make.

His verbatim words above are untouched.
