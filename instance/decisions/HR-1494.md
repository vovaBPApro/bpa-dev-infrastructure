## Ruling

1. When the operator asks for an estimate, he is asking for an estimate **under this
   infrastructure and this approach** — a fleet of lanes working in parallel under the
   current cap — not for how long the work would take one worker in one thread.
2. He identifies this as a **recurring** error, not a one-off, and asks in his own words
   for it to be written into an instruction. This file is that record.
3. An estimate must therefore state the assumed concurrency it was converted at. An
   estimate in lane-hours with no stated denominator is not an answer to his question.

## Why this is recorded two days late, and what that cost

Message 1494 was sent on 2026-08-02 and asked directly for this to be recorded. It was
not recorded. It sat untriaged in `instance/decisions/inbox.jsonl` for two days, among
175 other messages, until the operator ordered the inbox triaged ahead of the sprint on
2026-08-04.

In the meantime the error he named recurred, in the exact form he described. The
2026-08-04 cutover consilium's adversary member measured it: the standing estimate of
**170–230 lane-hours to cutover** had been converted using an assumed fleet of about six
lanes at 70% utilisation, while the system's own `fleet-nudge` log showed mean
concurrency of **2.42 lanes across 107 samples**, with the nominal floor of 10 met in
**zero of 483 firings**. The denominator never existed.

So the operator identified the defect, asked for it to be written down, was not heard,
and the same defect then produced the number the next two days of planning were built on.
That is the cost of an untriaged inbox, and it is the argument for HR-1494 being a rule
rather than a preference.

## How to apply

- State the concurrency assumption with every estimate, and prefer wall-clock under the
  **current** cap (HR-2342: three lanes) over an abstract lane-hour total.
- A reviewer is a lane. Three lanes means at most two coder lanes plus a review slot, and
  an escalated Tier-A row consumes two of the three.
- When the real fleet width is known from measurement, use the measured number rather
  than the configured floor. The configured floor has been wrong by 4x.
