# HR-2526 — score the board on what holds; let a consilium pick each sprint

Verbatim (Vova, Telegram 2526, 2026-08-05), correcting the orchestrator's proposal that
every row carry "what breaks if this is not done" and "what this unblocks":

> кожен рядок несе відповідь на "що зламається якщо цього не зробити" і "що це розблоковує" - це звучить добре, але ж це динамічна одиниця, саме тому ми закладали механізм того щоб збирати команду яка планує спрінт по борду, в моменті обирає що ми беремо в наступний спрінт і працює над ним. Якось цей підхід розмився і забувся. На мою думку що б допомогло - це один прохід по борду і оцінка складності задачі і її важливості, а далі рухатись спрінтами, і можливо там консиліум вирішить що є сенс зробити спрінт з найпростіших рядків, щоб розгрісти дрібниці які постійно заважають а вже потім рухатись з великими фічами

## Ruling

1. **"What breaks" and "what this unblocks" are dynamic and must not be frozen into rows.**
   The orchestrator proposed exactly that and he rejected it. A dependency answer written in
   August is wrong in September, and a stale answer read as current is this repository's
   most expensive recurring defect.
2. **Score once on the two properties that do hold: difficulty and importance.** These are
   comparatively stable, they are cheap to assign, and together they are enough to sort a
   board of 113 rows into something a planner can work with.
3. **Then move in sprints, and let a consilium choose each one from the scored board, in the
   moment.** Selection is a judgement made against current state — it belongs to a planning
   session, not to a field written once.
4. **A sprint of the simplest rows is an explicitly legitimate outcome** — his own example:
   clear the small things that constantly get in the way before taking on big features. The
   consilium may decide that, and the orchestrator must not treat "important" as the only
   axis worth acting on.

## The failure he is naming, which is the load-bearing part

> Якось цей підхід розмився і забувся.

The consilium-plans-the-sprint mechanism **was already agreed** and stopped happening.
Verified while recording this: `grep -rn "sprint" instructions/*.md` returns **nothing** —
there is no L2 mechanism for sprint planning anywhere in the instruction set. The approach
lived in decisions and in whoever remembered it, which is precisely the shape this phase
exists to remove.

So this ruling is not only a planning preference. It is another instance of the class the
requirements audit found: an agreed thing that reached no mechanism, and therefore decayed
into nothing without a single failure being visible. It belongs to Phase 5 for that reason,
not merely because it concerns the board.

## Consequence for work already in flight

Lane `ag-v3-5.14` was dispatched **before this arrived**, carrying the superseded framing —
each row to record what breaks and what it unblocks. A systemd lane takes no new instruction,
which is the same situation HR-2451 was written about.

It is **not** being killed, and the reason is specific rather than convenient: its expensive
work is deciding, with a SHA, which rows are **already closed**. That determination is
independent of the scoring axes and is the bulk of the value. The `open-important` /
`long-box` split it produces is superseded by this ruling and will be replaced by the
difficulty/importance scores in a follow-up pass over its output.

If that follow-up does not happen, this note is the evidence of what was dropped.
