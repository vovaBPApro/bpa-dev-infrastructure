## Ruling

1. **The cap of three is per repository, not global.** Each repository may run up to three
   lanes concurrently. HR-2342's number is unchanged; only its scope is.
2. **His stated reason is part of the ruling:** more lanes on one repository means more
   merge work into master, and merge work costs tokens. Three per repository keeps
   development close to linear, so lanes do not spend their budget resolving each other.
3. **"Наша мета зараз — не швидкість, а надійний і впевнений рух у правильному
   напрямку."** Reliability outranks throughput. Where the two conflict, choose the one
   that is easier to verify, even when it is slower.
4. **Tonight is infrastructure; product is decided in the morning.** He is not yet
   convinced the infrastructure is finished — *"Я відчуваю що інфра щє не до кінця
   готова!"* — and that judgement is his to make.

## What this does not license

The per-repository scope does **not** raise the host's capacity. HR-2342's measurement
stands: this box saturates around five concurrent lanes (12 cores, load average 16.46), and
the test suite stretches under load until deadlines kill passing runs — so lane count above
real capacity corrupts the evidence the fleet exists to produce.

Three repositories running three lanes each is **nine**, which this host cannot carry. The
per-repository cap is a rule about *merge topology*, not about hardware. Until a derived
budget exists (V3-0.34), the host limit is the binding one and the orchestrator must keep
the **total** within measured capacity, allocating between repositories rather than
summing caps.

Today the question is theoretical: one repository exists. It stops being theoretical the
day a product repository does, and that is also the day Hard Rule 6's single-repo boundary
sunsets by its own terms (`sunset: phase != sole-mission`).

## How to apply

- One repository: no change from HR-2342 — three lanes.
- Two or more: three per repository as a ceiling, **and** the total still bounded by
  measured host capacity. When those conflict, the host wins and the orchestrator says so
  rather than quietly exceeding it.
- Prefer finishing a repository's work over spreading thin across repositories. Linear
  progress with fewer merges is the stated goal.
