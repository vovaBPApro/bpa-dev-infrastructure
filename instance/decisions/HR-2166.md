## What it requires

Today HR-1726 caps review at three rounds and the row then parks. The operator's
ruling inserts a phase before the park:

1. Three review rounds, unchanged.
2. If the row is still not accepted, the lane gets **up to three further rounds
   whose purpose is proof, not redesign** — run the tests, produce debug logs,
   reproduce the reviewer's finding on its own SHA. The same practice a product
   coder follows: the person who wrote the change demonstrates it works.
3. Only if it still fails review does the row park and come to the operator —
   which the operator explicitly confirms is correct behaviour and should stay:
   *"ти його не доробляєш, просто йдеш до мене і питаєш."*

## Why it is right, in the orchestrator's words rather than the operator's

Every rejection recorded on this installation so far has been rejected for
**missing or unexecuted evidence**, not for a wrong idea. V3-0.15 round 1: a
documented command that could not run as written. V3-0.20 round 1: six findings
closed and one measurement unreproducible. V3-0.23 rounds 1 and 2: a bound whose
own locks ran at a budget where the defect was arithmetically impossible.
V3-0.29 round 3: four blocking findings, all outside what the new locks assert,
with both suites green. V3-1.9a rounds 1 and 2: the destructive path never
executed against a real account.

In every one of those the design survived and the evidence did not. A phase that
spends rounds on proving rather than rewriting is therefore aimed at the actual
failure mode, and it is cheaper than a recut: the work already exists.

## Boundaries

- A proving round **may not change the decision under review**. If a proving round
  concludes the design is wrong, that is a finding and the row goes back to
  redesign — it does not silently become round four of the same argument.
- A proving round is still subject to Hard Floor 7. Producing a log is not
  producing a pass; an unmeasured subject stays unmeasured.
- The park is not removed. It moves.
- This does not change who may release a park. That is V3-0.29, and its round-3
  review found the current mechanism unsafe.

### The trial

**Window**: from 2026-08-04 09:12 UTC to **2026-08-05 09:12 UTC**, one day.

**Verdict at the end**: if the challenges that were hard to close are closed with
this approach *and then test properly*, the practice is confirmed, written into
the product plan, and the implementation is copied across rather than rebuilt —
the operator's point being that by then it is already implemented and tested here.

### What is measured, stated now so the trial cannot be graded after the fact

A trial with no stated measure is an unmeasured subject, and Hard Floor 7 says an
unmeasured subject is never a pass. So:

- **Subject set**: rows that reach the review cap *with the design intact* and
  therefore enter proving rounds.
- **Helped** means: the row reaches ACCEPT and lands within the window, and the
  reviewer's closure rests on evidence the proving rounds produced.
- **Made it worse** means: proving rounds consumed budget and the row still parked,
  or a proving round changed the decision under review — which this ruling forbids
  and which would show the phase is being used as extra redesign rounds under
  another name.
- **Not enough subjects** is a permitted outcome and must be reported as such
  rather than dressed up either way.

### An honest caveat recorded at the start, not at the end

The three rows past the cap right now — V3-0.23 (round 3), V3-0.29 (round 4),
V3-0.30 (round 5) — are **redesign** rounds, not proving rounds. Their reviews
found the design wrong, not the evidence missing: a trust root that reads a local
ref, a stall watchdog armed to decide later than the runner containing it, and a
rule with no upper bound in time. Under this ruling's own boundary those go back
to redesign.

So the first genuine proving round has not happened yet, and none of these three
counts as evidence for or against the practice. Saying this at the start is the
point: it is what stops the trial being graded on rows it never applied to.

### Carrying it to the product

`instance/params.yaml` is `phase: sole-mission` and no product repository exists,
so there is no product mission artifact to write into yet. The requirement is
recorded here with its promotion trigger: **when the first product repository is
created, this practice is part of its mission from the start** — the coder proves
its own change, review comes before proving, and the cap escalates rather than
parking straight away. Hard Rule 6's single-repo boundary sunsets on the same
event, so the two move together.
