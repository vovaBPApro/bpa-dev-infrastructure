# HR-2285 — a review round is charged by a reviewer's judgement, never by paperwork

Recorded 2026-08-04 from Telegram 2283 and 2285.

## How it came up

`V3-0.55` — the change that makes the meteorite name the failing test instead of
only the failing stage — was **parked at `no-progress`** after three landing
attempts. None of the three was a judgement about the work:

1. the first went to diagnosing the meteorite itself;
2. the second was refused at `LAND verify-count mismatch report=14/0 actual=586/0`
   — the lane had declared a subset run;
3. the third did not reach a verdict.

So the row that would have made every later failure diagnosable was blocked, and
blocked for bookkeeping.

## The ruling

**A round is charged only when a reviewer examined the change and rejected it.**

Refusals that happen *before* anyone judged the work do not count:

- report shape, missing or malformed fields, an unusable `verify-count`;
- a review artifact naming a superseded SHA after a rebase or a re-issue;
- freshness — a base that moved while the lane worked;
- lock contention, and gate-internal aborts such as a rolled-back merge.

**And, at the operator's explicit qualification: none of this relaxes the gate.**
Bad paperwork is still refused. Sloppiness still costs the lane a full re-run —
its time, its tokens, another walk through the gate. What changes is only that it
no longer moves the row toward a park.

The reason to separate them, stated so a later reader does not re-merge them: a
park must mean **"we could not prove this is good"**, not **"we kept mishandling
the forms"**. Those need different responses — the first needs a redesign or an
operator decision, the second needs a better mechanism.

## What this makes unnecessary

`V3-0.55` should not need a hand-issued unpark. Under this rule it never
accumulated three rounds, because no reviewer rejected it. Applying HR-2149-style
authority to release it would have been treating the symptom.
