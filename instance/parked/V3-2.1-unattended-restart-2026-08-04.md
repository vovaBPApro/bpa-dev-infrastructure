## What is genuinely closed — verified by execution, keep all of it

- Arming is **observed** by a real disposable systemd manager, not requested: real pids,
  measured `elapsed_ms` against a configured interval, container torn down by an EXIT
  trap. This is the trap that parked V3-2.3 and this row avoided it.
- Both `is-enabled` and `is-active` predicates fail closed independently
  (`bootstrap/install.sh:437-446`) — either 2026-08-01 state would now be caught.
- A persistently failing orchestrator reaches a visible `failed` state rather than a
  silent restart loop.
- The lock derives its unit from the **tracked template**, with a single named
  `RestartSec=1` timing seam; mutating `Restart=` or `ExecStart=... supervise` in the
  shipped template turns it red.
- Round 3's split is the right shape: `Restart=on-failure`, `RestartSec=10`,
  `StartLimitIntervalSec=60`, `StartLimitBurst=3` and `ExecStart=... launch.sh supervise`
  are asserted **before** capability classification, so a clean container still proves
  the shipped unit is configured to recover even where it cannot watch it happen.

## The open blocker

`tools/shell-test-tier.test.ts:82-93` (especially `88-93`);
`instance/expected-shell-capability-exclusions.tsv:2`.

The bidirectional exclusion lock is false-green **in the disappearing direction**. The
executor derives `byFile` only from the inventory, so deleting the sole
`bootstrap/systemd-recovery.test.sh` inventory row means that file is never executed
during the comparison — its still-emitted exclusion becomes invisible and the tier
passes.

This is the enumerate-versus-absence trap that already produced V3-0.8 and V3-1.2: **a
checker that enumerates what exists cannot detect a deletion.** It reappeared here one
level up, in the mechanism that tracks exclusions rather than the mechanism that tracks
tests.

## The lock the recut must satisfy

The tier must go red when a tracked exclusion **disappears**, not only when an untracked
one appears — which requires comparing against the tracked list independently of what
the inventory happens to execute. Prove both directions by mutation: add an exclusion
without listing it, and delete a listed exclusion's inventory row.

Related: V3-0.8, V3-1.2 (the same trap, one layer down), V3-0.21 (the rebuild gate that
caught round 2 — its first prevented regression), V3-2.3 (parked on the *requested*
versus *observed* arming distinction this row got right).
