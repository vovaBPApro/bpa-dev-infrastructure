The quoted block is the orchestrator's proposal, which he quoted back. **His own words

Sent minutes after he said, in message 2358, that a week had gone into infrastructure and
arrived nowhere, and that he wants to build the product.

## Ruling

1. **The infrastructure is not to be finished. It is to be bounded.** Work on it only
   where it blocks working on the product. Everything else on the workboard becomes
   reactive — fixed when it actually obstructs, not because it was discovered.
2. **The bounded minimum**, after which work moves to the product:
   - the orchestrator starts from a clean clone of `main` (today it does not — it runs
     only on two lines in a gitignored file);
   - the fleet watchdog is alive and wakes the orchestrator (dead since ~17:53 today);
   - the meteorite **starts** the orchestrator and asserts a live state, instead of
     asserting that files copied.
3. Estimated at **13–26 lane-hours** — one night and a day at the three-lane cap, against
   8–16 working days to take the board to "cutover-ready".

## Why this overrides the sprint programme written hours earlier

`instance/consilium-cutover-2026-08-04-evening-synthesis.md` sets a goal of making
cutover-readiness decidable across gates A–G. That goal stands as a **definition**; it no
longer sets the **scope of work**. Gates that do not block product work are measurements
to report, not tasks to schedule.

The reasoning that produced this ruling is worth keeping, because it is the argument
against the repository's own self-hosting thesis:

- The board takes on ~32 rows a day and closes 10–12. The system finds defects in itself
  faster than it repairs them, which is what "digging in the wrong place" felt like to the
  operator and is arithmetic rather than mood.
- Self-hosting justifies that cost by compounding: fix a bottleneck once, every lane
  benefits. At ten lanes that repays. Under HR-2342's **three**-lane cap — with a reviewer
  occupying one slot and an escalated Tier-A row taking two — the multiplier is close to
  1, so the compounding argument that justified "infrastructure first" is now weak.
- Therefore the honest move is not to finish the infrastructure but to stop paying for
  leverage that no longer exists.

## How to apply

Before scheduling any infrastructure row, ask: **does this block product work today?** If
not, it is reactive. Report it, do not schedule it. A row that has been open for days
without blocking anything is evidence for this ruling, not against it.
